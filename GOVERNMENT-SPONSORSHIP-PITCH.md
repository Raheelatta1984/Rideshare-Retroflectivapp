# Retroflex: Digital Rideshare Identification

**A stationary-first Android display concept for a Transport for NSW co-design and feasibility pilot**<br>
**Discussion draft — 9 October 2026**

> **Purpose:** Seek a Transport for NSW (TfNSW) sponsor and co-design partner to assess a working prototype, define the regulatory and technical pathway, and—only if approved—shape a small, controlled pilot. This is an independent project concept. It is not endorsed by TfNSW or any rideshare platform, and it does not claim regulatory approval or compliance.

## Slide 1 — Retroflex

### Digital rideshare identification, designed to be blank while moving

A driver-controlled Android app paired with an Android display terminal. The proposed display presents an approved rideshare identifier only when the driver enables it and the vehicle is confirmed stationary.

- **Working prototype:** demonstration available
- **Seeking:** a government sponsor and co-design partner for a scoped feasibility study and, subject to approval, a controlled pilot

*Independent project concept. Not an official TfNSW or rideshare-platform product or endorsement.*

## Slide 2 — A practical opportunity, if it can be proven safe

Physical rideshare stickers provide a familiar identifier, but they are static and manually managed. A digital display could add a controlled, testable layer—without displacing any required physical identifier.

### The opportunity to assess

- **Today:** Rideshare platform identification is commonly provided by a physical sticker or other fixed identifier.
- **Question:** Can a driver-controlled display make the permitted identifier easier to manage while remaining dark during vehicle movement?
- **Potential:** A supplementary digital identifier with a defined operating state, a low default brightness, and a fail-blank response.

**The digital display is proposed as a companion—not a replacement for any legally required sticker, plate, or identifier unless formally approved.**

---

## Slide 3 — One Android app. One paired in-vehicle terminal.

### Proposed system

1. **Driver app — Android mobile**<br>
   The driver enables or disables the display, selects an authorized identifier, and can check terminal power/charging status and brightness.

2. **Remote control — internet connection**<br>
   The app sends display commands to the paired terminal. Authentication, command integrity, service availability, and outage behavior must be independently assessed before a live pilot.

3. **Display terminal — Android mobile or tablet**<br>
   Shows the approved identifier to the intended audience. Mounting location(s)—including any front- or rear-facing configuration—must be agreed after visibility, distraction, and occupant-safety review.

**Pilot principle:** retain all required physical rideshare identification while the digital concept is being assessed.

---

## Slide 4 — Display state follows vehicle state

### Proposed operating logic for validation

1. **Driver enables the display.** The driver can switch it off at any time.
2. **Vehicle remains stopped for more than 10 seconds.** The authorized identifier is shown.
3. **Speed exceeds 0.5 km/h.** The display automatically changes to a blank screen.
4. **Speed input, connection, or system fault is detected.** The safe-state proposal is a blank screen until the system is healthy and the stationary condition is re-established.

- **Default brightness:** 10%, with final settings subject to visibility and night-time assessment.
- **No interaction required while moving.** The motion threshold, data source, response time, and failure behavior require independent testing.

*These are prototype/proposed operating parameters—not a determination that the design complies with NSW law.*

## Slide 5 — A pilot should measure benefits, not assume them

The public value proposition is a hypothesis to test with drivers, passengers, technical reviewers, and relevant agencies.

- **Pickup clarity:** Can participants correctly identify the intended rideshare vehicle, and how long does identification take?
- **Motion-state reliability:** Does the screen blank promptly and consistently when movement begins? Does it only return after the agreed stationary period?
- **Driver experience:** Is the app easy to use before a trip, and does the display create distraction, glare, or other concerns?
- **Operational performance:** How reliably do pairing, charging, brightness, connectivity, and fault handling work in real conditions?

**No safety, efficiency, or customer-experience benefit should be claimed until the pilot has been independently evaluated.**

## Slide 6 — Why sponsor the feasibility stage?

A small, conditional investment can generate decision-quality evidence before any larger commitment.

- **Evidence before deployment:** independently assess road safety, vehicle fit, cybersecurity, privacy, usability, and the approval pathway before any on-road trial.
- **Public-interest learning:** measure pickup clarity, driver experience, and fail-blank performance with an agreed, opt-in evaluation—not assumptions.
- **Manage risk early:** use written decision gates and independent review to identify issues before the project scales.
- **No lock-in:** sponsorship of feasibility work is not a commitment to procure, endorse, or roll out the system; any next phase is a separate go/no-go decision.

**Sponsorship would fund learning and risk reduction. Any deployment decision would remain evidence-led and subject to formal approval.**

## Slide 7 — Safety, privacy, and cyber review come first

### Road and vehicle safety

- Confirm the applicable road-rule, vehicle-standard, signage, and approval pathway with TfNSW and relevant authorities before any public-road use.
- Assess driver field of view, visual distraction, glare/day-night legibility, screen brightness, mounting, wiring, occupant safety, and failure states.
- Keep the conventional required rideshare identification in place during any approved pilot.
- Limit the first phase to authorized rideshare identification—no third-party advertising, QR codes, video, animation, or audio.

### Cybersecurity and privacy

- Independently review device pairing, authentication, authorization, command integrity, data protection, software updates, logging, and backend access.
- Conduct threat modelling and penetration testing; resolve critical findings before any live pilot.
- Define safe behavior during lost connectivity, invalid commands, speed-signal loss, and device faults.
- Minimize data collection. Avoid passenger identity or precise location data unless essential, consented, and formally approved; define retention and incident-response procedures.

*These are proposed controls and work items. Their presence and effectiveness must be verified; no legal-compliance or security-certification claim is made.*

## Slide 8 — Three gates from demonstrator to evidence

### 01 · Co-design and feasibility

- Appoint a TfNSW sponsor and cross-functional contacts.
- Map requirements, applicable rules, stakeholders, risks, and approval dependencies.
- Agree independent safety, privacy, and cyber reviewers.

### 02 · Bench and off-road verification

- Validate motion thresholds, blanking latency, stationary reactivation, fault behavior, brightness, power, mounting, and device compatibility.
- Complete cybersecurity and privacy assessments and close critical findings.

### 03 · Controlled, opt-in evaluation

- Proceed only after written approvals and agreed operating conditions.
- Use a jointly selected cohort, approved vehicle/display configuration, clear consent, monitoring, and a stop/rollback process.
- Retain required physical identifiers; collect only agreed, minimized evaluation data.

**Go/no-go criteria, pilot size, duration, and location should be defined with TfNSW—not assumed in advance.**
